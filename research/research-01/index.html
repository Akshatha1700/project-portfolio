The question, as one sentence

This page is a scaffold, not a write-up. The layout is the house template for research notes; every dashed box is a prompt and every monospaced field is a detail still to be supplied.

To supply — the finding in one sentence

The answer you did get, including how partial it is. A finding that says "partly, and here is where it stops" is more useful than one that overstates. If there is no answer yet, delete this box and say so in the status row above.

Fill-in checklist — delete this box when the page is finished
  1. The question in the title, and the five rows of the spec table above.
  2. Every section below: replace the prompt with your own prose.
  3. Fig. 1: swap the plate for a real figure, or delete it.
  4. The data table — fill it in, or delete it.
  5. The references list at the end — add real sources, or delete it.
  6. This box, last of all.

Everything on this site is hand-written HTML. There is no build step and no templating, so deleting a block is always safe.

The question

To supply — 2 to 3 paragraphs
  • The question, and why reading alone did not answer it.
  • What you expected to find, before you looked.
  • What the scope deliberately excludes.

How it was approached

To supply — 2 to 4 paragraphs

What you did, in enough detail that somebody could follow it and get a different answer. Name the tools and the stopping point.

A note on margin notes This aside floats out into the margin on wide screens. Use it for a definition, an aside or a caveat that would otherwise interrupt the paragraph.

What the data is

To supply — provenance and licence, or delete this section

Where the data came from, how much of it there is, what you had to fix by hand, and under what licence you are allowed to use it. A reader who cannot get the data cannot check the finding.

What came out of it

To supply — 2 to 4 paragraphs, plus a figure if you have one
  • The result, stated plainly and first.
  • How confident you are, and why.
  • Anything that surprised you, including results you did not want.

Where it stops

To supply — the honest caveats

Sample size, stopping point, assumptions that might not hold, and the alternative explanation you could not rule out. Stating these costs a paragraph and buys the reader's trust.

Notes and references

To supply — real references, or delete this section

An ordered list. Link papers, documentation and datasets directly and give the access date — a URL with no date is a claim nobody can check later.
