The brief

To supply — 2 to 4 paragraphs
  • What the problem actually was, as somebody described it to you.
  • Who had the problem, and what they were doing instead.
  • Why the obvious fix did not work, if it did not.

The first paragraph is set in the larger lede size, so it should be the one that carries the whole section.

What I built

To supply — 2 to 4 paragraphs

What the thing does, described for somebody who will never see it running. Name the parts that were decisions rather than defaults.

The hard part

To supply — 2 to 4 paragraphs

The section a reader actually came for: the thing that did not work the first time, and how you found out. Concrete beats general — what you assumed, what the measurement said instead, what you changed.

A note on margin notes This aside floats out into the margin on wide screens. Use it for the aside that would otherwise interrupt the paragraph — or delete it and use the numbered notes at the end instead.

What I measured

To supply — before and after numbers, or delete this section

If you have real measurements, fill in the table. State the dataset, the number of runs and the machine, because a number without those is not a measurement. If you have none, delete the section — an invented benchmark is worse than no section at all.

What I would do differently

To supply — a short list, one per line

Three or four items, each starting with the lesson in bold and followed by what it cost you. If you have none, you have not looked hard enough.

Notes and sources

To supply — real references, or delete this section

Numbered notes for anything a reader might want to check: documentation, a paper, a dataset, a caveat that does not belong in the body. The original layout used three; use as many as are genuinely load-bearing.
